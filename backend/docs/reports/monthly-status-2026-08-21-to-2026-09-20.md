## Executive summary

During this period, the project advanced from core platform and procurement workflow implementation into broad agent coverage and formal assurance. The strongest dated evidence is the Phase 1 dry-run UAT on 9 September and the Phase 2/3 dry-run UAT report dated 11 September. Together, those reports record 26 agents exercising a total of 165 cases, all marked passed. The Phase 2/3 report also records 21 red-team attack classes and 19 gate records.

These results establish useful implementation and dry-run evidence; they do not demonstrate that the agents produced correct results from live business records or safely completed production transactions. The Phase 1 lanes remained in dry-run in the UAT procedure, and the Phase 2/3 gate table says live promotion was pending. Five agents have no corresponding phase UAT report in the supplied evidence. The daily deliverables workbook has blank status cells, so planned tasks cannot be treated as completed solely because their target dates passed.

## Key risks and issues

1. **Dry-run is not live-data validation.** The recorded Phase 1 dry-run suppresses agent tools and connectors. Passing it proves input validation and task/ledger tracing, not business-result accuracy against live records.
2. **Five agents lack dated phase UAT evidence.** They need test packs and documented business review before they can be considered validated.
3. **Production promotion remains open.** The Phase 2/3 report identifies external-data and setup prerequisites, including Zoho/CRM data, approved WhatsApp templates, HR rows, DealerMart data, and AMC contracts.
4. **The schedule baseline is inconsistent.** The Daily Deliverables workbook covers 20 weeks from 27 July to 11 December; the WBS describes a 24-week program starting 4 August. Progress should be measured against one approved baseline.
5. **Progress reporting is weak.** The workbook’s status cells are blank, and no Git history is present in the workspace. It is not possible to produce a reliable earned-completion percentage or prove when every current file was completed.
6. **The 31-agent suite and eight-assistant specifications are not fully reconciled.** Confirm which higher-level commerce/customer assistants are included in this project’s acceptance scope.

## Priorities for the next reporting period

1. Approve a single scope, owner map, schedule baseline, and requirement-to-evidence tracker for the 31 agents and the eight assistant specifications.
2. Complete documented UAT for P2, P3, F2, S2, and S3; use representative business records and owner-reviewed expected outputs.

4. Resolve and verify integration prerequisites end to end: Zoho data reads, Tally connection and test-company posting, approved WhatsApp templates and sandbox recipient, HR input data, DealerMart build, and AMC contract data.
5. Run end-to-end workflows across agent boundaries in a sandbox, including purchase-to-receipt-to-invoice matching, collections-to-books reconciliation, campaigns-to-response analytics, and service/AMC renewals.
6. Publish a per-agent gate report that distinguishes **implemented**, **unit/golden tested**, **live-data shadow tested**, **sandbox write tested**, and **supervised-live approved**. Do not count a health check or placeholder dry-run as a live-data pass.
7. Re-baseline launch dates and complete operational readiness: monitoring, rollback rehearsal, runbooks, training, KPI reconciliation, and owner sign-off.
